Shipping a small feature every week beats shipping a perfect feature once a quarter. Small releases are easier to review, easier to roll back, and they keep feedback loops tight.

## Why small wins

When a change is small, you can reason about the blast radius. A 50-line pull request gets a thoughtful review. A 2,000-line one gets a rubber stamp. The math is simple: smaller diffs mean fewer surprises in production.

## My weekly rhythm

1. **Monday** — pick one outcome, not five tasks.
2. **Mid-week** — put something behind a flag or on staging.
3. **Friday** — release, observe, and write down what you learned.

## Measure the outcome, not the output

Lines of code and commit counts are vanity metrics. What matters is whether the release moved the number you care about: conversion, load time, error rate. If it did not move, the lesson is still a win — you learned cheaply.

> If it takes more than a week to get feedback, the batch is too big.

Small, steady, observable. That is the whole game.
